\begin{problem}
  If you took \textsl{Physics I} with Hogg, you know he loves a particular problem with two pulleys and three blocks, of mass $3\,\kg$, $4\,\kg$, and $7\,\kg$.
  Hogg will draw this problem on the board in Lecture and discuss the relevant equations.
  In the end, the acceleration $a_7$ of the $7\,\kg$ block can be found by solving a set of $N$ equations in $N$ unknowns.
  Encode the equations in the form $A\,x=b$, where $A$ and $b$ are known quantities, and the unkowns are in $x$, and solve it with \code{numpy.linalg} linear algebra.
  What do you get for the dimensionless acceleration $a_7 / g$ for the big block?
\end{problem}

\begin{note}
  The point of this problem is the computational part, not the first-year-physics part.
  So get your equations from Hogg or your buddy if you like.
  This isn't about writing down the right equations; it is about putting them correctly into a computer and solving them.
\end{note}

\begin{note}
  Computers don't take care of units (well actually they can, but \code{numpy.linalg} knows nothing about units).
  Thus the best way to solve this problem is to \emph{make it dimensionless}.
  What does that mean in this case?
  You don't have to make the problem dimensionless to solve it here, but your approach is more general if you do.
\end{note}
